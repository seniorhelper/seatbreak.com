Our mission

Our goal: leave your mind a little better than we found it.

SeatBreak is a functional game. Fun comes first. The learning is built in, so you practice real skills while you play. This is our aim, not a promise or a tested result.

Play SeatBreak Say hello

Learn by playing

We believe practice builds skill. SeatBreak is designed to give you practice at noticing, reasoning and staying calm, with real science along the way. We hope players carry some of that into the real world we all share. We have not run studies on it, so we do not claim results.

  • Logic and deduction

    How you practice it in the game

    Every room is a chain of clues. You notice, compare, test an idea and try again. Codes, balances, clocks and light mixing all reward careful thinking.

  • Real science

    How you practice it in the game

    After each escape you see the real science you just used: levers, buoyancy, light, Morse code and more. We check every fact against a source.

  • Calm under pressure

    How you practice it in the game

    The timed vault asks you to stay steady and think. There are no jump scares, no strobing lights and no flashing red. A hint is always one look away.

  • Seeing is not believing

    How you practice it in the game

    The Mind Bender uses classic optical illusions, so you learn how your own eyes and brain can be fooled, and why that is fun.

  • Seated and open to everyone

    How you practice it in the game

    Play from a chair, a recliner or a table. No walking, and no controllers needed: look at things to use them. Built for people who cannot or do not want to stand.

Gentle by design

No blood, no gore and nobody gets hurt. The rooms are puzzles, not fights. The best way through is to look closely, think, and try again.

Everything you see and hear in SeatBreak is original. The art, textures, music, sound effects and the voice of Bolt, your drone guide, are all made in code for this game.

Zach's philosophy

AI for good: the data center that gives back

Picture the data centers of the future. Their waste heat warms homes and organic greenhouses. Reverse-osmosis (RO) cleaning makes rainwater safe to reuse. Trees are planted, and a nature reserve grows around the site.

We start with the design.

Concept design. Not an engineering or construction document.

The whole system in words

The site has one data hall, where rows of servers work and make heat. Cold air reaches the servers from one side and hot air leaves on the other, so the heat is gathered in one place. A liquid cooling loop carries that heat to a heat-exchanger plant. The exchanger passes the heat to a separate loop, so the server liquid never mixes with anything else.

A heat pump station then lifts that gentle warmth to the temperature district heating needs. Two insulated pipes leave the station. The warm supply pipe runs to a street of homes. The cooler return pipe collects the water, passes through organic greenhouses so they get warmth too, and goes back to the heat pump cool.

Rain from the roofs is caught and stored. Reverse osmosis cleans it. The clean water goes to a storage tank, then to greenhouse irrigation and to top up the cooling plant. The water left over from cleaning flows to a constructed wetland that polishes it, and then to a pond in a nature reserve.

A solar canopy over the car park and a battery store are designed to supply part of the site's power. Tree belts and reforestation blocks surround the site. A wildlife corridor lets animals pass through. A learning center lets kids and families see how it all works. Everything here is a concept and a design target, not a finished plan.
